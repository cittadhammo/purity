# The Weaponization of Purity: How Moral Contagion and Ostracization Fuel Authoritarian Dynamics

Across modern digital spaces—from software repository issue threads to social media commentary—a distinct phenomenon regularly unfolds: the immediate, absolute rejection of functional tools, art, or infrastructure based entirely on the personal political identity of their creators. When an open-source tool like David Heinemeier Hansson’s *Omarchy* distribution faces immediate social boycotts, or when consumer products are vilified based on the public statements of executives like Elon Musk, the underlying conflict is rarely about the code or the physical engineering. Instead, it represents a deep-seated psychological dynamic: **the prioritization of ideological purity over utility.**

While many online groups view these boycotts as a modern, decentralized way to hold people accountable or stand up against bad actors, history and sociology reveal a striking paradox. The very habits driving today's digital exile—viewing tools as morally contaminated, demanding total political alignment, and aggressively policing others—are the exact same mindsets that historically paved the way for classical fascism and authoritarian control.

## I. The Psychology of Moral Contagion and the F-Scale

To understand why a user would reject a piece of software or a physical good created by an ideological opponent, one must look to the cognitive architecture of **moral contagion**.

In moral psychology, human beings frequently merge physical disgust (a survival mechanism evolved to avoid pathogens) with moral disgust (a social mechanism evolved to enforce group boundaries). When an individual operates under high ideological purity, an object or tool ceases to be a neutral artifact composed of logic or materials; it becomes symbolically "polluted" by the creator’s identity. As social psychologist Jonathan Haidt notes in *The Righteous Mind*:

*"When sacred values are threatened, people react with moral outrage, tribal cohesion, and a total suspension of pragmatic calculation."*

This suspension of pragmatic calculation was thoroughly investigated following World War II. In their landmark 1950 study, *The Authoritarian Personality*, Theodor W. Adorno and his colleagues developed the **Fascism Scale (F-Scale)** to quantify the psychological traits that predispose populations toward totalizing systems. Rather than finding that fascism was merely imposed from above by military force, Adorno’s empirical research revealed that authoritarianism relies on specific traits widely distributed throughout general populations:

1. **Rigid Conventionalism:** Unyielding adherence to designated group norms and moral purity tests.  
2. **Authoritarian Aggression:** A compulsive drive to publicly condemn, ostracize, and punish anyone who violates those norms or belongs to an "out-group."  
3. **Anti-Introspection:** A complete intolerance for nuance, ambiguity, or self-critique, rendering the world into a strict binary of "pure vs. corrupt."

When these traits dominate, the concept of a "neutral tool" disappears entirely.

## II. Totalizing Ideology and the Destruction of Private Utility

Classical fascism and totalitarianism are distinguished not simply by state violence, but by their **totalizing ambition**—the belief that no area of human life can remain politically neutral. Benito Mussolini famously synthesized this doctrine in his 1925 address:

*"Everything in the State, nothing outside the State, nothing against the State."*

Under a totalizing framework, art, engineering, language, and everyday commerce are stripped of their independent value and reassigned as political indicators.

### Historical Precedents of Tool-Politicization

* **Nazi Germany and "Degenerate Art" (*Entartete Kunst*):** In the 1930s, the Nazi regime did not merely critique modern art; they declared it morally toxic to the body politic. The technical skill of the painter or composer was irremediable if the artist was Jewish, Marxist, or politically non-compliant. Utility and aesthetic excellence were entirely subordinated to racial and ideological purity.  
* **Soviet Lysenkoism:** Under Joseph Stalin, classical genetics—developed in large part by Western scientists—was declared "bourgeois pseudoscience." Stalin’s regime promoted Trofim Lysenko’s flawed agrarian theories because they aligned with Marxist-Leninist doctrines of environmental determinism. Agricultural utility was sacrificed for ideological purity, contributing directly to widespread crop failures and famine.

In both cases, the belief that "the author contaminates the product" led to the outright destruction of real-world utility in favor of symbolic conformity.

## III. The Enforcers: "The True Believer" and Denunciation Culture

Totalitarian systems cannot operate solely through top-down state coercion. They rely fundamentally on a critical mass of ordinary citizens willing to enforce conformity through social exile and public denunciation.

In his seminal 1951 work *The True Believer: Thoughts on the Nature of Mass Movements*, philosopher Eric Hoffer demonstrated that the psychological driver behind radical movements is rarely the specific substance of the cause itself, but a desperate need for collective certainty and an identifiable enemy to persecute:

*"A rising mass movement attracts and holds a following not because its doctrine and promises can satisfy the desire for self-advancement, but because it can satisfy the passion for self-renunciation... For the fanatic, fanaticism is an instrument of self-forgetting, and his passions are fed by the conviction that his cause is sacred."*

Hoffer highlighted how "true believers" routinely swap one radical ideology for another—such as former communists joining the Nazi party in 1930s Weimar Germany—because their primary gratification comes from the **feeling of moral purity and the right to enforce social exiled status on dissenters.**

### The Engine of Social Ostracization

Historians of 20th-century authoritarianism, such as Hannah Arendt and Ian Kershaw, have demonstrated that formal state apparatuses (like the Gestapo or NKVD) were remarkably small relative to the populations they controlled. Historical records reveal that the vast majority of political investigations were initiated by ordinary citizens filing voluntary denunciations against neighbors, colleagues, and rivals to demonstrate their own ideological alignment.

The social mechanism preceded the legal state mechanism:

> Moral Contagion → Public Ostracization / Boycott → Enforced Conformity → Institutional Purges

## IV. The Modern Irony: Anti-Fascist Rhetoric via Authoritarian Dynamics

The central paradox of modern digital purity culture is that those who engage in aggressive ostracization often explicitly identify as "anti-fascist" or advocates for social justice. They view public call-outs, deplatforming, and boycotts as necessary tactics to combat perceived authoritarian threats.

However, political sociology highlights a profound disconnect between **stated intentions** and **behavioral structure**:

| Dimension | Classical Authoritarian Impulse | Extreme Digital Purity Culture |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **View of Neutrality** | "Neutrality is treason." | "Silence is violence / Neutrality supports the oppressor." |
| **View of the Product/Art** | Must reflect official state ideology. | Must reflect correct social/political stance of creator. |
| **Method of Enforcement** | State purges, public denunciation, exile. | Deplatforming, social exile, career cancellation, boycotts. |
| **Cognitive Framing** | Strict binary (Pure vs. Degenerate). | Strict binary (Aligned vs. Complicit). |

When an activist group insists that using an open-source tool, reading an author, or buying a product is an act of moral compromise, they are adopting the exact structural logic of the authoritarian systems they claim to oppose. They grant themselves a moral blank check: because their *ends* are declared righteous, their *means*—coercion, social punishment, and intolerance—are excused.

## V. Pragmatism as the Ultimate Antidote

Societies remain resilient against totalitarian decay only so long as they preserve **pluralism and pragmatic utility**—the understanding that a tool's value is distinct from its creator's soul, and that human beings can collaborate on functional tasks without sharing identical worldviews.

The pragmatic stance does not require endorsing the political opinions of every software maintainer, manufacturer, or artist. Rather, it recognizes that decoupling the *product* from the *author* is a fundamental requirement for a free, complex, and functionally diverse society. When a culture allows ideological purity to dictate which code may be run, which books may be read, or which bread may be eaten, it has already laid the psychological trackwork for the very authoritarianism it fears.
