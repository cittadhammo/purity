When an open-source software project, a piece of engineering, or a work of art is discarded simply because of who made it, the issue is rarely about the work itself. Instead, it signals a deeper psychological rift: the moment ideological purity overrides functional utility.

**The Purity Paradox** explores why human beings regularly sacrifice superior tools, pragmatic cooperation, and real-world value to enforce moral boundaries. Across four interconnected essays, this collection traces how ancient biological adaptations—originally evolved to protect small hunter-gatherer bands—interact with modern digital networks to fuel social exile, online call-outs, and totalizing worldviews.

By examining the neurobiology of moral disgust, the evolutionary origin of norm-enforcement, and historical precedents of totalizing movements, these essays reveal why a hyper-vigilant minority enforces strict group purity—and how societies can build resilient, pluralistic spaces that safeguard functional utility for everyone.
